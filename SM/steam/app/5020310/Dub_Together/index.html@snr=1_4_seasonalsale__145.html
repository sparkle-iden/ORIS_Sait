About This Game

In Dub Together you redub video clips together. Up to eight players speak their lines into their own microphone and turn a scene everyone knows into something completely new.

Once all the recordings are done the curtain goes up and you watch together what came out of it.

Parallel Mode: Everyone dubs the same clip

Everyone gets the same clip and makes their own version of it. At the end you pick the best one.

1. Record

The clip plays without the original voices. You see your text and speak it to fit the scene, line by line, without hearing what the others are making of it.

2. Watch

The curtain goes up and you watch every version in turn over the original picture.

3. Vote

Everyone picks a favourite. The best version goes on the podium. Afterwards every version can be saved as a video.

Character Mode: Everyone voices one role

Instead of eight separate versions you end up with a single one that everybody worked on. Two players or more.

1. Assign the roles

Every character in the clip gets one performer. Roles nobody takes keep their original voice.

2. Record together

You go through the lines together. Whoever is up speaks, the others watch. That way the dialogue grows out of the conversation instead of running side by side.

3. Watch

You watch your shared version in one piece and save it as a video. There is no vote here, there is only one result.

This goes for both modes: a recording you are not happy with, you simply do again, as long as the round timer is running.

Give your voice a new sound

You can pick a voice effect for every single line. Turn into a squeaky voice with Chipmunk, into a villain with Monster, or make things even more absurd with Echo, Robot and Phone.

The effects are applied straight to your recording. On playback everyone hears exactly the version you performed.

A built-in noise filter removes background noise between the sentences and makes sure that simple microphones still come out clean.

Turn your own clips into rounds

The Pack Creator turns your own videos into playable rounds.

Just drop a clip in and trim it down to the part you want. The spoken parts are detected automatically and the matching text is written for them. Speech, music and background sound are separated from each other, so the atmosphere of the scene stays in while you replace the voices.

Finally you assign a character to each line, export the pack and can play it right away.

Share your packs on the Steam Workshop

Upload your finished packs to the Workshop with one click, or subscribe to other people's. Whatever you subscribe to shows up in your list right away, no restart needed.

If a player is missing the pack for the round, their game pulls it in by itself when the round starts. Nothing has to be sent back and forth.

For streamers and their community

Connect your Twitch channel and let your chat decide along with you.

Viewers vote for their favourite performance with !vote or simply the number, and get to influence who wins the round. Streamer mode also hides the lobby code on screen so nobody drops into your session uninvited.

At a glance

  • Online multiplayer for up to eight players

  • Two modes: Parallel with a vote, Character with fixed roles

  • Join through the Steam friends list or with a lobby code

  • Your own voice recordings with six different voice effects

  • Automatic speech and text detection for your own clips

  • Steam Workshop for sharing and subscribing to packs

  • Twitch integration with viewer votes

  • Export the finished dub as a video

  • Light and dark theme

Packs made by players

Dub Together ships with a Pack Creator, and players share their packs through the

Steam Workshop. Those packs come from the players themselves and live on Steam's

servers, not on ours.

You may only upload material you hold the rights to. For films, shows or music by

others you need permission from the rights holders. If you spot a pack that does

not belong there, report it on its Steam page or write to support@dub-together.com.

We will look into it.

One pack ships with the game: an excerpt from Sprite Fright by Blender

Studio, used under CC BY 4.0.

AI Generated Content Disclosure

The developers describe how their game uses AI Generated Content like this:

Some of the artwork in this game was made with AI image generators and edited by hand afterwards. This covers the two player characters, the logo, the menu icons and the curtain texture. The English interface texts were written with the help of a language model and reviewed afterwards. The German texts are written by hand.

The bundled Pack Creator uses a speech recognition model to find the spoken parts of a video you supply yourself and turn them into the lines you read out. An AI noise filter for your microphone recordings and AI-based separation of speech and background audio work the same way. All of this runs locally on your own machine and never sends data to an external service. These models only process material you provide yourself, they do not invent content of their own, and the result stays inside your own lobby.
